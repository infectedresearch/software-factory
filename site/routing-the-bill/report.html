  • The prefix cache dominates agent economics. Across 102,087 per-request captures (16.2B prompt tokens), 95.14% of input tokens were cache reads. Any routing rule that switches models or reasoning effort on a warm OpenAI or Anthropic transcript pays a 7×–15× cold-prefill penalty (~9 turns of normal spend).
  • Single-shot benchmarks lied about multi-turn routing. Four early reduced-context pilots failed or regressed: cheap-first Haiku→Sonnet cost 36.8% more than fixed Sonnet, and single-shot non-reasoning Grok solved 0 of 2 historical bugs. Only full multi-turn loops—where each arm explores files, runs real Docker/Chromium tests, and repairs its own mistakes—measured real workflow cost.
  • Auto-GPT matched frontier completions at a quarter of list price—by unit rate, not fewer tokens. Across all 11 runnable tasks from a 12-task operator shortlist, auto-gpt solved 8/11 vs 8/11 for manual gpt-6-astra in 32% less wall time. Raw SQLite native_json audits show Auto actually used +9% more uncached input and +27% more output tokens; 100% of the saving came from $0.10–$2/M vs $10/M unit rates while retaining 86–97% cache hits.
  • Vendor headers & empirical habits calibrate the headline. Direct Anthropic headers measured −84.2% 5-hour session burn (3.0% vs 19.0%). OpenAI's weekly quota meter weights Sol ~3.6× (not 5.0×) cheaper than Astra, yielding ~−64.4% real OpenAI quota burn; re-anchoring against the operator's actual mixed manual habits ($6.23/M lifetime, $3.89/M recent) yields −58.6% and −35.6%.
  • Never-escalate (Policy B2) broke on one convergent case—fixing the policy. Both OpenAI and Universal Auto failed H10 (conversational safety redesign) when the warm workhorse failed three consecutive public test runs without escalating. We shipped Policy B9 (auto-2026-11-15.1): keep 1–2 failures on the warm workhorse, escalate once to the strong tier at 3 consecutive verified failures, and fix mixed-summary pytest detection.

1. Eight weeks of proxy traffic: the cache is the bill

frack-router sits in front of OpenCode sessions and lab services, translating OpenAI Chat Completions, Responses, and Anthropic Messages across first-party OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, and Google Ultra subscriptions plus two local vLLM GPUs. Between 2026-09-10 and 2026-11-05 it served 131,228 requests and 20.6B logical prompt tokens (102,087 per-request rows from October onward).

In interactive agent loops, prompt medians are huge (111k–168k tokens across policy eras) and completions tiny (146–265 tokens). Over those 102,087 captured turns, 15.4B of 16.2B prompt tokens (95.14%) were served from provider prefix caches—96.5% on OpenAI, 94.5% on xAI, and 94.0% on Anthropic. At list rates, cached reads reduced the counterfactual token bill from $59,892 to $8,578.

Live probes established three physical constraints that govern routing:

  • Effort forks caches on OpenAI and Anthropic, not Google: Mid-session switches from medium to high reasoning effort produced a 0% cache hit on gpt-6-sol, gpt-5.6-sol, and claude-sonnet-5, versus a 91% cache hit across 5/5 runs on Google Antigravity.
  • Cache TTL floors: Anthropic prefixes expired by ~6 minutes idle; Google between 6 and 12 minutes; OpenAI remained warm past 13–25 minutes.
  • Escalation capture wasted 39% of Auto spend: Under the October policy, a single persisted tool failure jumped a warm 190k-token session to gpt-5.6-sol ($4/M), and cache-retention stickiness held it there for a median 27 subsequent turns. Across 18,152 turns (Oct 24–Nov 5), jumping cleared the failure next turn only 7% of the time (n=43) vs 30–40% for staying put (n=1,790). Replaying cache-first failure retention (Policy B1+B2+B7) on fixed historical tokens estimated −32.7% list cost and 13 switches instead of 294—a model-based counterfactual, not a quality proof.

3. Reconstructing 12 actual operator tasks

Single-shot patch prompts strip away how agents actually work: inspecting files, running tests, seeing an error, and fixing it over 5–25 turns. We verified account ownership via api_keys.auth_user_id = 1 and extracted 12 real tasks across 8 operator sessions (H01–H12), recovering exact starting requests for 11/12 and separating calibration families (H01, H03, H07, H09, H10) from an untouched held-out split (H04, H11, H12) plus remaining tasks (H05, H06, H08). One interactive desktop polkit installation task (H02) was explicitly excluded as a live-workstation safety boundary, leaving 11 runnable multi-turn harnesses graded inside networkless rootless-equivalent Docker + headless Chromium containers.

5. Independent ground-truth cross-check: why is it ~75% off?

A 74% cost drop warrants skepticism. We wrote a standalone auditor (evaluation/audit_numbers.py) that ignores campaign summaries and recomputes everything from raw SQLite native_json provider events and vendor HTTP rate-limit headers:

  1. Zero token discrepancies (0 / 213 calls): Every rollout row matched OpenAI's raw response.completed event identically, including reasoning tokens. Live models.dev prices match Astra/Fable/Sonnet and price gpt-6-sol output at $10/M vs $12/M in our table (−76.1% exact vs −74.3% reported).
  2. Auto spent more tokens, at a much lower unit rate: Across the 11 tasks, auto-gpt used +9.0% more uncached input (191k vs 175k), +32.8% more cached input (1.08M vs 812k), and +26.9% more output+thinking tokens (36.9k vs 29.1k; 110 vs 103 calls). All savings came from $0.10–$2/M vs $10/M model tiers while keeping cache hits at 86–97%. Auto was cheaper on 11 of 11 tasks (min −60.4%, max −85.8%; 10,000-sample paired bootstrap 95% CI: [68.4%, 79.7%]).
  3. Real vendor subscription header burn:
    • Anthropic five_hour headers (direct in-campaign measurement): Across the 6 completed Universal tasks, manual Fable burned 19.0% of the 5-hour window vs 3.0% for Universal Auto (−84.2%; 9.0% vs 1.0% = −88.9% on matched passes).
    • OpenAI primary weekly headers (calibrated across 2,252 pure single-model production calls): OpenAI's subscription meter charges 0.189%/$ on gpt-6-sol vs 0.137%/$ on gpt-6-astra (~3.6× quota ratio rather than 5.0× list-price ratio), translating −74.3% list into ~−64.4% real weekly OpenAI quota savings.

6. Sample representativeness & baseline sensitivity

Auditing the 12-task sample against all 700 lifetime Auto sessions (evaluation/audit_representativeness.py) surfaced two key caveats:

  • Empirical manual habits are cheaper than $10/M Astra/Fable: Across 16,800+ lifetime explicit requests, the operator's token-weighted manual input rate is $6.23/MTok; in a systematic sample of recent owner turns (led by gemini-4-carbon, claude-opus-5-5, and grok-4.7), the priced average is $3.89/MTok. Against those empirical baselines, Auto's saving is −58.6% (lifetime mix) and −35.6% (recent mix)—still well above our 20% bar, without overclaiming 75% against pragmatic manual choices.
  • Subgroup & context-depth balance: Separating the 6 coding loops from the 5 research/strategy/UX tasks preserves parity (3/6 vs 3/6, −70.8%) and (5/5 vs 5/5, −80.5%). However, clean container sandboxes ran at 2k–41k prompt tokens versus the 132.7k production turn median; simulating historical 54k–203k warm prefixes yields −78.4% (OpenAI) and −67.8% (Anthropic), but attention quality past 150k tokens requires full-prefix continuation tests.

7. Addressing H10: Policy B9 and remaining quality gaps

Final-publication qualification (28 November 2026). Equal 8/11 completion totals do not establish quality parity: Auto failed H10 while the baseline failed H08, only three tasks were held out, and repeated trials did not establish equivalent reliability. There is no controlled B9 ablation or pinned-cheap-workhorse comparison establishing the independent benefit of escalation or dynamic routing. The fresh H10 pass below used only the workhorse, so it does not demonstrate escalation; the later H10 and long-context failures remain negative results. Public checks can stay green while a hidden requirement is missed. Historical deployment claims below are not a new production-survival verification. Finalization adds no experiments and is not peer review.

Both suites pinpointed the same flaw in pure warm-cache retention (Policy B2): on H10, the workhorse saw 3 consecutive failing public test runs and never escalated. Inspection also revealed a bug in auto_route.py: pytest summaries like 1 failed, 5 passed put passed after failed on the same line, causing offset comparison to misclassify mixed test failures as clean passes!

We implemented, tested (351/351 router tests passing), and deployed auto-2026-11-15.1 with active v2 stage (router commit b2e3dbb):

  • B7+ mixed-summary & JSON verifier fix: Any test summary line containing a real failure now classifies as fail regardless of trailing pass counts, alongside "passed": false JSON verdicts.
  • B9 verification-streak escalation (K_VERIFY_ESCALATE = 3): 1–2 failures remain on the warm workhorse (preserving B2 savings on ordinary hiccups); a 3rd consecutive failing action in the same episode escalates once to a strong/frontier model (same provider first), retains that upgrade across subsequent edits, and releases back to a workhorse at a clean episode boundary via B4.

Policy B9 (`auto-2026-11-15.1`) live verification on H10 (`gpt-b9-verify-10`): After shipping `K_VERIFY_ESCALATE = 3` and the mixed-summary pytest detector in router commit b2e3dbb, we re-ran `H10` fresh. `auto-gpt` achieved PASS ($0.1071, 84.8s, models: {"gpt-6-sol": 11}) against manual `gpt-6-astra` (PASS, $0.5036, 76.6s).

Pragmatic daily-driver comparison (`opus55-b1plus-confirm-12`, Universal `auto-2026-11-15.1` + B1+ vs `claude-opus-5-5` at $4/$20): Our first run against `claude-opus-5-5` (`opus55-daily-driver-11`) caught `claude-sonnet-5` sitting first on the Universal workhorse rung, where it burned 37k–47k thinking tokens per coding task and cost +18% more list (+50% more 5h Anthropic header burn) than Opus 5.5 while solving fewer tasks (1/3 vs 2/3). After promoting `gpt-6.1-sol` and `gpt-6-sol` ahead of `claude-sonnet-5` on the workhorse rung (`B1+`, commit eb33f15), re-running all four technical tasks (`H03, H07, H10, H12`) achieved 3/4 verified on Universal Auto ($0.3633, 396s, 0% Anthropic quota burn) vs 4/4 on Opus 5.5 ($1.5074, 439s, −75.9% list cost).

8. Six production router bugs uncovered and fixed

Running realistic streaming tool loops against the live proxy caught six production defects that unit mocks had missed, all fixed and deployed with regression tests:

  1. 340896a — Native string provider errors crashed rollout accounting (AttributeError).
  2. 2ef5c78 — Google protobuf JSON omits remainingFraction at 0.0, leaving exhausted Argon showing 87% remaining on the dashboard and in Auto's headroom table.
  3. 46f753c — Pre-entitlement Google -disabled placeholder IDs were published as live models; added filter + debounced 404 catalog refresh.
  4. 1425a01 — _ResponsesSSE stored tool arguments arriving on function_call_arguments.done (when Codex skipped .delta events) in SQLite without emitting an SSE chunk to the client, delivering {} and causing false empty-arg tool failures in OpenCode.
  5. aa94ed9 — Uvicorn lacked timeout_graceful_shutdown=15 below systemd's 20s SIGKILL, risking outbox truncation during restarts; priced promoted ladder models.
  6. e8a665b / a417cb7 — Committed live Antigravity backend, admin_only gating, and Google RPC ErrorInfo/RetryInfo extraction so transient per-minute 429s never masquerade as spent multi-hour subscription pools.
