CLOSING NOTES · 28 NOVEMBER 2026

What our agent gates failed to measure

Task cards made building cheaper. Proof lines sometimes proved the wrong thing. A cheaper routing policy stayed green while missing a requirement.

Infected Research / FrackLabs

These are closing notes for the software-factory and model-routing experiments. The original reports remain the experimental record; this page draws out three results and separates them from a later implementation audit. It adds no new benchmark run. The visual-generation report is a separate experiment, not evidence for the conclusions below.

1. We made implementation cheaper by moving work out of implementation

In the second factory run, seven lanes hunted for their own work and seven consumed scout-written task cards. Card passes took a median seven minutes instead of 14.5. Model spending on implementation fell from $271.44 to $91.12.

But scouting cost another $147.86. Writing plus scouting per merged non-minor commit was $4.18 for free lanes and $4.27 for cards. The expensive part had moved upstream. Of the scouting bill, $62.31 produced no cards: stalled sessions, a wrong working directory, and a quota interruption.

The queue also changed integration. Twenty of 68 card-arm survivors did not apply automatically; none of 69 free-arm survivors had that problem. Manual conflict resolution recovered much of the card arm's work. The approval step accepted 76 cards, rejected none, and left 18 unreviewed. It introduced waiting without demonstrating selection value.

This is not a verdict against planning. It is a warning about the accounting boundary. The arms worked different areas; human approval and conflict-resolution time were not priced. The small observed difference is not evidence of equivalence. The pre-registration gives a writing-cost formula while separately requiring scouting to be charged: both the builder-only and combined results should be read, rather than pretending that wording was unambiguous. See the original criteria and per-arm data.

2. Failing on the old revision is necessary evidence, not automatically a regression proof

The gate required a Proof: line. Independent checkers then sampled 16 of 137 review survivors and ran the named tests against the parent and candidate. All eight sampled free-lane proofs tested behavior; only four of eight card-lane proofs did.

Two card proofs failed on the parent because a new module or constant did not exist there. That demonstrates a dependency on new code, not necessarily the alleged defect. Another proof claimed a fail-before result although the commit only added a test. The test could be useful; the claim about what it established was not.

A proof check has to ask why the parent failed. A missing symbol, a broken fixture, and the old production behavior violating a requirement are different outcomes. In the inspected factory implementation, the gate still checks proof metadata and delegates execution to review. The proposed automatic semantic parent-versus-candidate check was not established by this audit. Recommendations in a paper are not shipped features.

3. Our cheaper router used more tokens, and its verifier still had a blind spot

In Routing the Bill, the 11-task paired Auto-GPT arm used more uncached input, more cached input, more output/thinking, and more calls than manual Astra. Reported list cost nevertheless fell from $4.0189 to $1.0345. Both completed eight tasks, but they did not fail the same tasks; only three tasks were held out.

The saving came from model prices while retaining reusable context, not from consuming fewer tokens. Against the recent observed manual model mix, estimated savings narrowed to 35.6%. List-price estimates, subscription allowance, and cash paid are different quantities.

Cheap-first was not sufficient. A six-call Haiku-first pilot cost 36.76% more than fixed Sonnet. An initial Sonnet-first comparison solved one of three tasks against two of three for Opus while costing 18% more. Those failures are part of the result, not discarded setup.

The sharpest limit appeared in a continuation with a 433-message prefix, approximately 90k tokens. Auto passed all three public checks but eight of nine overall; Astra passed nine of nine. Auto cost $0.4316 versus $2.0721 and took 81 seconds versus 122. A policy that escalates after repeated visible test failures cannot rescue a requirement its visible verifier misses.

Cache-first routing remained active in the 25 November implementation audit. The three-failure escalation rule remained in code, but the audit did not demonstrate its causal contribution to success. A later fix also had to reset failure streaks after a passing result. There is no demonstrated quality-parity guarantee, and no shown ablation establishing that the router beats pinning the same cheap workhorse.

What remained after the experiments

The 25 November audit found proof metadata, separate review, browser evidence packages for eligible UI work, scout proposals, and human acceptance still in the implementation. Scout and worker timers were enabled; that does not prove a recent proposal completed. Cache-first policy had current active routing telemetry.

Some individual fixes remained useful but needed repair. A factory presence change preserved richer display metadata; a later correction preserved the right identity when combining phone and web presence. A username-lookup expression index remained in code, although the audit did not run a live query-plan check. Both factory rounds also left safety-filter findings as known-failing tests before follow-up sessions repaired them.

The final round-two report includes a qualified history check: 130 of 131 integration commits mapped through cherry-pick trailers; no explicit reverts or linked Fixes: follow-ups were found, and 51 mapped commits had later production-file touches. This narrow linkage check misses unlinked remediation, including known filter follow-up. It is not a production survival rate.

Where this stops

The experiments are complete for this release. Round three was prepared but not run. The artifacts are retained for inspection and adaptation, not offered as a supported general-purpose service. Initial counts stay frozen; publication finalization does not mean peer review, independent replication, or fresh production verification of every change.

The reusable result is a set of concrete distinctions: builder cost versus end-to-end cost; test failure versus defect evidence; public checks versus the complete requirement. We obtained useful fixes and cheaper routing. We also obtained examples of success signals that were too narrow to justify the claims we wanted to make.
